Report Season: noticing strain early, a psychological health and safety program
Primary school teachers and team leaders

Learner ______________________   Assessor ______________________   Date ____________

Part A · Multiple choice

Choose the best answer.

1. Priya says she’ll be fine once Friday is done, but she’s sleeping five hours and snapped at a student. What is the best response?
  1. Hang in there, we all go through it
  2. Sit down, check in properly and encourage her to raise the workload with the team leader
  3. Write her remaining comments yourself tonight
  4. Say nothing; it’s private
2. Why is “I’ll do it for you” not the best long-term response?
  1. It is unkind
  2. It hides the workload problem and moves the hazard to you
  3. It is against policy
  4. It takes too long
3. What is a psychosocial hazard?
  1. A personal weakness
  2. Something in the design or management of work that can cause psychological harm
  3. A physical injury
  4. A disagreement with a colleague
4. Which opening invites a real answer?
  1. You okay?
  2. Busy, huh?
  3. You seem stretched lately. How are you really going?
  4. Cheer up
5. Who can change workload?
  1. Only the individual teacher
  2. The team leader or principal, with the team
  3. Students
  4. Nobody

6. Describe two early signs that a colleague may be under strain.
7. Explain what you would say to encourage a colleague to raise workload.

Part C · Scenario

It is week 8 of term 2. A colleague has stopped coming to the staffroom at lunch and emails you at 11 pm about reports. When you ask, she says, “It’s just that time of year.”

What would you do? Explain your actions and why.

Marking guide (for assessors)

Part A answers

  1. B. Sit down, check in properly and encourage her to raise the workload with the team leader It notices the signs and raises the hazard with someone who can change the work.
  2. B. It hides the workload problem and moves the hazard to you Taking on the work quietly hides the problem from the people who can fix it.
  3. B. Something in the design or management of work that can cause psychological harm Sustained high workload is one example.
  4. C. You seem stretched lately. How are you really going? It names what you have noticed and gives permission to answer honestly.
  5. B. The team leader or principal, with the team Workload is managed through the people who design and allocate the work.

Part B model answers

  1. Less sleep; irritability; withdrawing; working late every night; joking it off; missing breaks.
  2. Acknowledge what you have noticed; offer to go with them; frame it as a work issue the team leader can help with.

Part C: look for

  • Names what they have noticed without judgement
  • Asks an open question and listens
  • Encourages raising workload with the team leader, and offers support
  • Shares support contacts

Result: all Part D criteria observed, and Parts A to C answered to the standard above. Where something is not yet shown, give feedback and agree a time to reassess.
