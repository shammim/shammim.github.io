Facilitator guide

Care That Counts: 30-day aged care onboarding program
New care workers in residential, memory support, home care and respite

SessionDignity and choice in personal care
Length60 minutes
Group4 to 12 new care workers

Purpose

This session sits inside the program after the eLearning. It turns what learners saw in the course into practice they can use on the job.

Learning outcomes

  1. Uphold residents’ rights, dignity and choice in everyday care
  2. Act in line with the Code of Conduct for Aged Care
  3. Recognise and report serious incidents and changes in a resident’s condition
  4. Support safe mealtimes and respond calmly to distress in dementia care

Before the session

  • Participants complete Stage 1 and 2 screens first
  • Invite an experienced care worker to share practice
  • Check your organisation’s SIRS and escalation procedures

Materials

  • Dignity and Choice Card
  • Scenario cards (Mrs Okafor’s shower, Mrs Lee’s clothing choice)
  • Statement of Rights summary
  • Learner workbook

Run sheet

TimeActivityFacilitator notes
0:00What does dignity look like?Ask each person for one moment they felt respected receiving care, of any kind.
0:10Rights in plain wordsMatch situations to rights in the Statement of Rights (Workbook activity 1).
0:20Role-play: “after lunch, please”Mrs Okafor prefers to shower after lunch, and a colleague says there isn’t time. Practise responses in threes.
0:35Consent and choicePractise asking, offering choices and checking in during care.
0:45When to reportReview what counts as a serious incident and who you tell, using your procedure.
0:55CloseEach person chooses one dignity habit to try with their buddy.

Discussion prompts

  • How do you offer a real choice when time is tight?
  • What would you say to a colleague who says “there isn’t time”?
  • What would make you report something straight away?
  • How do you know someone is consenting during care?

Facilitation tips

  • Frame rights as everyday actions, not rules.
  • Follow your organisation’s incident and SIRS procedures.
  • If someone discloses abuse or neglect, stop and report through your procedure.

After the session

Send participants the follow-up tasks in the pre- and post-program activities, and share the manager embedding guide with their managers.
