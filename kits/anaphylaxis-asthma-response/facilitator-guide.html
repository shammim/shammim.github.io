Facilitator guide

Anaphylaxis and Asthma Emergency Response: first aid officer program
Lead educators and nominated first aid officers in early childhood services

SessionEvery second counts: emergency drill session
Length2 hours
Group4 to 12 educators

Purpose

This session sits inside the program after the eLearning. It turns what learners saw in the course into practice they can use on the job.

Learning outcomes

  1. Tell a mild to moderate allergic reaction from anaphylaxis using the ASCIA Action Plan
  2. Give an adrenaline autoinjector correctly and call an ambulance without delay
  3. Respond to asthma using the child’s action plan, and treat anaphylaxis first when both are possible
  4. Record the incident and notify families and the regulatory authority as required

Before the session

  • Confirm every participant holds or is booked into approved first aid training
  • Gather trainer devices and expired-plan examples
  • Agree room roles with the room leader before the drill

Materials

  • Adrenaline autoinjector trainers (both brands used in your service)
  • Sample ASCIA Action Plans for anaphylaxis and allergic reactions
  • Asthma reliever, spacer and trainer
  • Quick Response Card
  • Incident, injury, trauma and illness record form
  • Timer

Run sheet

TimeActivityFacilitator notes
0:00Why minutes matterRecap how fast anaphylaxis progresses. Ask: what would slow you down in your room?
0:15Read the planPairs read three ASCIA plans and highlight the signs that mean give adrenaline now.
0:35Hands-on with trainersEveryone practises each trainer device until the steps are automatic. Peer check with the card.
1:00Lay flat, do not standPractise positioning, including a child who is having trouble breathing (sit, do not stand or walk).
1:15Whole-room drillRun the lunch-table scenario. One person leads, one calls 000, one supports the other children.
1:40Records and notificationsComplete the incident record together. Review notification timeframes in your service policy.
1:55CloseEach person names the one step they will rehearse this week.

Discussion prompts

  • Which sign on the plan means adrenaline straight away?
  • If you are not sure whether it is asthma or anaphylaxis, what do you do?
  • Who in your room calls 000, and where is the address written?
  • What do you record, and when?

Facilitation tips

  • This session supports, and does not replace, approved first aid training.
  • Always follow the child’s own action plan and your service’s medical conditions policy.
  • Use trainer devices only; never practise with a live autoinjector.

After the session

Send participants the follow-up tasks in the pre- and post-program activities, and share the manager embedding guide with their managers.
