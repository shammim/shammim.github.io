Facilitator guide

Safeguarding the Start: 30-day supervision induction
Graduate early childhood educators in their first 30 days

SessionEvery child in sight: supervision workshop
Length90 minutes
Group6 to 12 graduate educators

Purpose

This session sits inside the program after the eLearning. It turns what learners saw in the course into practice they can use on the job.

Learning outcomes

  1. Position yourself to keep every child in sight during room transitions
  2. Keep the required ratio for your room at all times
  3. Recognise threshold and transition risks before they happen
  4. Hand over supervision clearly before you step out

Before the session

  • Ask participants to finish the eLearning first
  • Print each participant’s room floor plan if you can
  • Invite a room leader to co-facilitate the role-plays

Materials

  • Printed floor plans of participants’ rooms, one per pair
  • Coloured dots for children and educators
  • Active Supervision job aid
  • Learner workbook
  • Timer

Run sheet

TimeActivityFacilitator notes
0:00Welcome and why it mattersShare a de-identified near miss. Ask: where were the adults standing?
0:10Map the roomPairs mark doors, blind spots and high-risk zones on a floor plan (Workbook activity 1).
0:25Transitions in slow motionReplay the pack-away transition from the eLearning. Pause at each decision: who is watching which zone?
0:45Role-play: the kitchen doorGroups of three. One educator is asked to step out. Practise the handover script while others scan.
1:05Ratios and the law in plain wordsReview NQS Element 2.2.1 and National Law s.165. Check: who counts toward ratio, and when?
1:20Commit and closeEach person writes one positioning habit for their next shift (Workbook activity 4).

Discussion prompts

  • Where can you stand to see both zones at once?
  • What tells you it is time to stop and recount?
  • What would you say if a colleague asks you to step out mid-transition?
  • Which part of the day feels riskiest in your room, and why?

Facilitation tips

  • Use participants’ own rooms wherever you can.
  • Keep the tone supportive: the aim is habits, not blame.
  • If a child safety concern comes up, pause and follow your service’s child safe policy.

After the session

Send participants the follow-up tasks in the pre- and post-program activities, and share the manager embedding guide with their managers.
