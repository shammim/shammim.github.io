Part A · Multiple choice

Choose the best answer.

1. Sam says he is “just a bit tight”, but his peak flow has dropped and he is breathing faster. What do you do?
  1. Accept his view and recheck at the next round
  2. Give his reliever as charted, then report the change promptly using ISBAR
  3. Wait to see if it settles
  4. Tell him to rest
2. What are the first steps of asthma first aid?
  1. Lie the person down and call the family
  2. Sit the person upright, give 4 separate puffs of reliever via a spacer, and wait 4 minutes
  3. Give the preventer
  4. Wait for symptoms to pass
3. When should you call an ambulance?
  1. Only if the person asks
  2. If the person is not improving after asthma first aid, or symptoms are severe
  3. After the family arrives
  4. Never; the doctor will decide
4. What is a preventer for?
  1. Quick relief during an attack
  2. Reducing airway inflammation over time when taken every day
  3. Only for exercise
  4. Replacing a spacer
5. In an NDIS setting, a participant declines to use their spacer. What do you do?
  1. Insist they use it
  2. Respect their choice, explain the benefit, follow their plan and record it
  3. Hide the puffer
  4. Call the family to make them

Marking guide (for assessors)

Part A answers

  1. B. Give his reliever as charted, then report the change promptly using ISBAR Treat within your scope, then escalate early. A falling trend needs another set of eyes.
  2. B. Sit the person upright, give 4 separate puffs of reliever via a spacer, and wait 4 minutes National Asthma Council asthma first aid: sit upright, 4 puffs, wait 4 minutes, repeat, call 000 if no improvement.
  3. B. If the person is not improving after asthma first aid, or symptoms are severe Call 000 straight away for severe symptoms or if there is little or no improvement.
  4. B. Reducing airway inflammation over time when taken every day Preventers are taken regularly to keep asthma under control.
  5. B. Respect their choice, explain the benefit, follow their plan and record it Choice and control matter; inform, follow the plan and document.

Part B model answers

  1. Night-time cough or waking; using the reliever more often; wheeze; shortness of breath; trouble speaking in full sentences; falling peak flow; restlessness or anxiety.
  2. Introduce self and role; situation (breathing, peak flow, SpO2); background (asthma, plan, medicines); assessment (worsening); recommendation (review now, plan read back).

Part C: look for

  • Recognises night-time symptoms as a warning sign
  • Follows her action plan and gives reliever within scope
  • Escalates to the nurse or doctor using ISBAR
  • Monitors and documents

Result: all Part D criteria observed, and Parts A to C answered to the standard above. Where something is not yet shown, give feedback and agree a time to reassess.
