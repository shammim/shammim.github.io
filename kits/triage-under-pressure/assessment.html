Part A · Multiple choice

Choose the best answer.

1. A caller is screaming that her husband has collapsed. What do you ask first?
  1. How old is he?
  2. What is the address of the emergency?
  3. Is he breathing?
  4. What medications does he take?
2. Which two answers set the priority in the first minute?
  1. Age and history
  2. Is the patient conscious? Are they breathing normally?
  3. Name and address
  4. Medications and allergies
3. The caller says the patient is “snoring” and won’t wake. What does this suggest?
  1. He is asleep and safe
  2. Abnormal breathing, which can signal cardiac arrest
  3. He has had too much to drink
  4. Nothing important
4. Why ask for the callback number early?
  1. For the records
  2. It’s the only way back if the line drops
  3. To check identity
  4. It’s optional
5. A caller is panicking. What works best?
  1. Telling them to calm down
  2. A calm, firm voice and one clear task
  3. Waiting until they stop
  4. Transferring the call

Part B · Short answer

6. Explain why location comes before anything else.
7. Describe how you would respond to “he’s breathing, sort of gasping”.

Part C · Scenario

A man calls from a busy shopping centre car park. His friend has collapsed between cars. He gives the centre name but not the level. There’s a lot of noise.

What would you do? Explain your actions and why.

Marking guide (for assessors)

Part A answers

  1. B. What is the address of the emergency? A crew can’t move until you know where to send it.
  2. B. Is the patient conscious? Are they breathing normally? Consciousness and normal breathing drive the priority.
  3. B. Abnormal breathing, which can signal cardiac arrest Snoring or gasping in an unresponsive person is not normal breathing.
  4. B. It’s the only way back if the line drops If the call drops, you need to call back.
  5. B. A calm, firm voice and one clear task “Calm down” usually escalates; a clear task gives control.

Part B model answers

  1. A crew can be assigned and moving while you continue the call; without it nothing can move.
  2. Treat gasping as abnormal breathing; code as cardiac arrest per protocol; start CPR instructions.

Part C: look for

  • Gets an exact location (level, bay, entrance) and reads it back
  • Gets a callback number
  • Asks conscious and breathing normally
  • Sets priority and gives one clear instruction

Result: all Part D criteria observed, and Parts A to C answered to the standard above. Where something is not yet shown, give feedback and agree a time to reassess.
