Family Conversation
Guide

Raising a concern without blame
EYLF V2.0 Partnerships · NQS Quality Area 6

OOpen well

Choose a private moment with enough time. Start with something positive and true.

“Ruby built an amazing tower today. Do you have five minutes, or is another time better?”
PPoint to what you observed

Share facts, not labels: what you saw, when and how often.

“At group time this week, Ruby pushed a child three times when they sat close to her.”
EExplore their view

Ask what the family notices at home. Listen more than you talk.

“What do you notice at home? Does Ruby like having her own space?”
NNext steps together

Agree on one or two small steps and when you’ll check in.

“Could we try her own cushion spot at group time? I’ll let you know how it goes on Friday.”

Strengths-based language

Instead ofTry
“He’s aggressive.”“He’s finding it hard to manage big feelings when the room is busy.”
“She’s attention-seeking.”“She’s looking for connection.”
“Your child won’t listen.”“We’re working on listening at group time.”
“He has to learn to share.”“We’re helping him learn to take turns.”

Good practice

  • Interpreters: use a professional interpreter for important conversations. Never ask the child or a sibling to interpret. Speak to the family, not the interpreter.
  • Document it: date, who was there, what you each shared, what you agreed and when you’ll follow up. Keep it factual.
  • When a parent gets defensive: it’s usually worry. Acknowledge their view, share one fact, offer one small change to try.

Pause and get support when

  • The family is very upset, or you disagree on next steps.
  • You need to plan a formal family meeting: invite your educational leader or nominated supervisor.
  • Avoid comparing home with the service, quoting policy, or talking about other families.

If there’s a safety concern, this isn’t a family conversation yet. Write down what you saw or what the child said, and tell your nominated supervisor straight away. Follow your service’s child safe policy and your mandatory reporting obligations. If a child is in immediate danger, call 000.
