Dignity and Choice
in Personal Care

Keep in your pocket or on the care trolley
Aged Care Quality Standards · Statement of Rights

1 · Knock and ask

Knock, say your name and ask before you come in or start. Ask even when a person lives with dementia.

“Hello Mrs Okafor, it’s Sam. Is now a good time for your shower?”
2 · Offer a real choice

Offer two options you can actually deliver, not an instruction dressed up as a choice.

“Would you like your shower now, or after lunch?”
3 · Protect privacy

Close the door and curtains. Keep the person covered and uncover only the part you are washing. Keep personal talk out of corridors.

“I’ll keep you covered. Tell me if you’d like the door closed more.”
4 · Go at their pace

Give time to answer. Let the person do what they can. Say what you’re about to do before you do it.

“Take your time. I’ll wait until you’re ready.”

A dignified shower in five steps

  1. Introduce yourself and say why you’re there, at eye level, in a calm voice.
  2. Ask permission and offer choices: timing, water temperature, who helps, what to wear.
  3. Prepare first so the person is never exposed: towels, clothes, soap and shower seat ready; doors and curtains closed.
  4. Talk through each step and check in. Watch their face and body, and stop if they look uncomfortable.
  5. Ask how it was and what they’d change next time, then record it in their care plan.

When the person says “not now”

Respect the answer, record it in their plan and offer a time you can keep. If the shift is stretched, tell your coordinator so help can be planned. The person’s choice stands.

Never

  • Hurry someone: “We haven’t got all day”
  • Choose their clothes because it’s quicker
  • Discuss toileting or personal care where others can hear
  • Skip asking because “nobody checks”
