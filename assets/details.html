At a glance

The challenge

Trainees need to understand safety protocols, biosecurity and workplace expectations before they set foot on site. The organisation also needed one consistent, accessible experience for every onboarding module in the series.

An end-to-end process: need, learner analysis, objectives, design, development and evaluation. Bite-sized chunks paired with authentic field scenarios.

Learn by doing

VET learner onboarding Workplace project

VET Onboarding: Your First 30 Days

Shows new VET students what their first 30 days look like, from enrolment to their first assessment, who to go to when something goes wrong, and what is expected of them.

Try the course

At a glance

Context
Vocational education and training
Audience
People starting a Certificate or Diploma with an RTO or TAFE, including first-time and returning learners
Format
Self-paced onboarding module on the series course template, SCORM 1.2 and 2004
Structure
3 modules, a practical scenario and a five-question final quiz, about 25 minutes
Resources
Day 1 to Day 30 timeline, 30-day checklist, glossary of VET terms, study habits and wellbeing
Accessibility
Voice-over, captions and transcript on every section; keyboard ordering with no dragging; shortcuts

The challenge

New VET students often don’t know what happens when, or who to ask. Small problems in the first month, like a login that fails, an assessment they don’t understand or a missed class, can quietly turn into a reason to leave.

The approach

The course is built around the first 30 days: a Day 1 to Day 30 timeline, the eight-stage journey from Enrol to Progress, and the four people and teams who can help. Each module ends with the learner applying it.

Content

Who can support you, the onboarding journey, learner responsibilities, study habits and wellbeing (with Lifeline and 000), and a glossary of VET terms such as RTO, USI, RPL and Not Yet Competent. It points learners back to their own provider’s policies.

Interactive features

A clickable 30-day timeline, support role cards, a put-the-journey-in-order activity using move buttons, two knowledge checks, Sam’s first-fortnight scenario with three moments, a final quiz (80% to pass), a checklist that saves ticks and a “first step” reflection.

Web skills for content teams Workplace project

HTML Essentials: Interactive Microlearning

Teaches content editors, marketers and new developers the core of HTML5, with a live code sandbox and a graded coding task in every unit.

Try the course

At a glance

Context
Workplace technology, web content teams
Audience
Staff who need to read, write or fix web pages at work: content editors, marketers, designers and new developers. No experience needed
Format
Rise-style interactive microlearning on a custom course template, SCORM 1.2 and 2004
Structure
5 units of 5 to 7 minutes and a final quiz (80% to pass), about 37 minutes
Resources
Practice files with starter pages and solutions, a two-page HTML5 quick reference and five job aids
Accessibility
Built to WCAG 2.1 AA; unit voice-over with captions and transcript; a code editor where Tab moves on, so keyboard users never get trapped

The challenge

Staff were editing web pages without knowing HTML. Big bold text stood in for headings, links said “click here”, images had no alt text and forms had no labels. Pages looked fine but failed screen reader users and search engines.

The approach

Learning HTML is a new alphabet and grammar at once, so each unit teaches one idea: see a worked example, try it in a live sandbox, check your thinking with a workplace scenario, then complete a short coding task.

Content

The page skeleton, headings and semantic structure, links and images with good alt text, lists and accessible tables, and accessible forms. Accessibility is built into every unit, not saved for the end.

Interactive features

Live code sandboxes with a preview and a heading outline, five coding tasks that check the learner’s code instantly and say exactly what to fix, flashcards, a sorting activity, Mei’s launch-review branching scenario and a final quiz.

Digital inclusion for older Australians Workplace project

Digital Skills for Seniors

Helps older Australians use a computer, video-call their family and spot scams, with support that fades from guided steps to free practice.

Try the course

At a glance

Context
Community, digital inclusion for older Australians
Audience
Older Australians who are new to computers or not yet confident online, learning at home or with a community program
Format
Self-paced course in a custom player that also runs offline from a single file
Structure
A welcome, 4 lessons and a wrap-up, 23 short screens, about 45 minutes
Resources
A printable summary card, plus where to get help in Australia: Scamwatch and Be Connected
Accessibility
Large text from the start with three text sizes, narration at about 130 words a minute with 0.75× speed, transcript on every screen, no timers and no tests to fail

The challenge

Many older Australians want to see their grandchildren on a video call, but feel nervous that they’ll break something or get scammed. Most training moves too fast, uses jargon and treats practice like a test.

The approach

Support fades as confidence grows. Lessons 1 and 2 are guided, step by step. In Lesson 3 learners choose the order, and Lesson 4 lets them pick their own practice. Every practice screen is safe: no real calls or messages are sent.

Content

The parts of a computer, clicking and the keys that do most of the work, opening and closing windows, answering and making a video call, strong passwords, spotting scam messages and AI voice scams, and the Stop, Check, Ask routine.

Interactive features

A practice desktop, a click target, a simulated video call from start to finish, safe-or-suspicious message sorting, a scam phone call scenario, a keyboard practice, before-and-after confidence check-ins and a personal summary card.

Project story

How it came together

  1. 1

    The brief

    Older Australians wanted to video-call family but were nervous about breaking something or being scammed.

  2. 2

    The key decision

    Fade the support: two guided lessons, then a lesson in any order, then free choice of practice, with no timers and no tests to fail.

  3. 3

    The build

    Safe practice screens for the computer, a full video call and scam spotting, narration paced at about 130 words a minute, three text sizes and confidence check-ins.

  4. 4

    The hand-over

    A 45-minute course that also runs offline from one file, with a personal summary card and links to Scamwatch and Be Connected.

Emergency call-taking · micro-learning Workplace project

Triage Under Pressure

A micro-learning module for emergency call-takers: lock the location, ask the big two and code the call within 60 seconds, on a simulated live call with caller audio and a decision timer.

Try the course

At a glance

Context
Emergency call-taking
Audience
Emergency call-takers and trainees (refresher or induction)
Format
Rise-style micro-learning with a branching call simulation · SCORM 1.2 and 2004
Structure
5 lessons and a final check, about 8 minutes
Assessment
Instinct check (baseline), branching call, final check 75% to pass
Accessibility
WCAG 2.1 AA: captions and transcript, keyboard operable, timer and sound can be switched off

Challenge & context

Under shift pressure, call-takers can misclassify a high-priority call or escalate a panicking caller. Two common traps: asking for history before locking the location, and hearing “snoring” in a collapsed patient as reassurance.

Instructional strategy

Action-mapped micro-learning: a baseline instinct check, the five-move question sequence, pressure traps and a sorting activity, then a two-minute live call against the clock and a debrief.

Content & evaluation

Built from a learning needs analysis of call logs, QA audit scores and supervisor debriefs. Kirkpatrick Level 1 and 2 are captured in the module, with Level 3 through a 30-day QA call audit.

Interactive features

A simulated CAD panel with six fields that fill as the call progresses, caller audio with captions, a call clock and a 20-second decision timer, and branching choices that each add seconds to the call.

Project story

How it came together

  1. 1

    The brief

    Under pressure, call-takers asked for history before location and heard “snoring” as reassurance.

  2. 2

    The key decision

    Action-mapped micro-learning: one behaviour, the first 60 seconds, practised against the clock.

  3. 3

    The build

    A baseline instinct check, five-move sequence, sorting and a two-minute live call with caller audio, a CAD panel and a decision timer.

  4. 4

    The hand-over

    An 8-minute SCORM module, a First 60 Seconds Quick Card and a 30-day QA audit plan.

Open LMS (Moodle) · course blueprint & assessment Workplace project

Dispatch Triage Pathway

A blended Open LMS pathway that takes dispatch trainees from a baseline check to a supervisor-verified call on the floor, and shows at every step whether the learning worked.

Try the course

At a glance

Context
Emergency dispatch training
Audience
Dispatch trainees (Part 1); L&D, operations and QA leads (Part 2)
Format
Open LMS (Moodle) blueprint, explained in a Rise-style module · SCORM 1.2 and 2004
Structure
Six Moodle sections; explainer module of 7 lessons and a final check, about 15 minutes
Assessment
Baseline and post-quiz on the same scale, feedback survey, 30-day supervisor checklist
Accessibility
WCAG 2.1 AA: plain English, high contrast, keyboard operable

Challenge & context

Dispatch trainees misclassify high-priority triage codes under shift pressure, and the organisation needs pre- and post-learning assessment that shows whether training worked on the floor, not just in the course.

Instructional strategy

A blended pathway: baseline checks, a branching SCORM scenario, a peer forum and live debrief, a post-assessment and certificate, then a supervisor checklist at day 30. Moodle completion rules gate each step.

Content & evaluation

Kirkpatrick Levels 1–3 built into the course page: baseline and post-quiz on matching scenarios and confidence items, a feedback survey, gradebook pass mark and a 30-day observation checklist.

Interactive features

An explorable Moodle course page with trainee and designer views, a certificate-unlock simulator, a pre/post confidence comparison and a working supervisor checklist with a competency verdict.

  1. 1

  2. 2

  3. 3

    The build

    A six-section Open LMS blueprint, matched pre and post assessments and a day-30 supervisor checklist, explained in an interactive module.

Multi-agency dispatch · performance support Workplace project

Four Stages to a Joint Response

A one-page job aid that turns multi-agency dispatch procedures into four visual stages, practised on a two-minute highway collision before dispatchers need it on shift.

Try the course

At a glance

Context
Multi-agency emergency dispatch
Audience
Call-takers, dispatchers and multi-agency coordinators; operations, QA and L&D leads
Format
One-page A4 job aid + Rise-style module with a branching scenario · SCORM 1.2 and 2004
Structure
8 lessons and a final check, about 17 minutes
Assessment
Baseline check, branching scenario, 10-question verification check, 90% to pass
Accessibility
WCAG 2.1 AA: agency colours tested for colour blindness, always with an icon and a label

Challenge & context

Multi-agency incidents broke at the handoffs: second and third agencies alerted late, triggers such as entrapment not escalated, and the same details re-keyed for each agency.

Instructional strategy

Performance support for the moment of apply: a one-page job aid in four stages, explored stage by stage, practised with trigger sorting, then used live on a highway collision with the aid one click away.

Content & evaluation

Built from incident logs, friction reports and supervisor debriefs across police, fire and ambulance dispatch. Evaluated with a baseline check, a reaction survey, a 90% verification check and a 30-day handoff audit.

Interactive features

An interactive job aid, METHANE step-through, trigger sorting, a CAD scenario with trigger selection, agency toggles, a METHANE form and a timed talkgroup patch, and a colour-blindness preview of the agency marks.

Project story

How it came together

  1. 1

    The brief

    Multi-agency incidents broke at the handoffs: late alerts, missed triggers and details re-keyed for each agency.

  2. 2

    The key decision

    Performance support first: a one-page job aid in four stages, used live in the scenario rather than memorised.

  3. 3

    The build

    An interactive job aid, METHANE practice, trigger sorting and a Highway 9 CAD scenario, with agency colours tested for colour blindness.

  4. 4

    The hand-over

    The A4 job aid, a module with a 90% verification check and a 30-day handoff audit.
