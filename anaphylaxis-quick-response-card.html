Anaphylaxis & Asthma
Quick Response Card

Post beside the medical cabinet
Follow the child’s own ASCIA Action Plan

Severe signs: anaphylaxis

  • Difficult or noisy breathing
  • Swelling of the tongue
  • Swelling or tightness in the throat
  • Wheeze or persistent cough
  • Difficulty talking or a hoarse voice
  • Persistent dizziness or collapse
  • Pale and floppy (young children)

Mild to moderate signs

  • Swelling of the lips, face or eyes
  • Hives or welts
  • Tingling mouth
  • Abdominal pain or vomiting (these are severe for insect allergy)

Stay with the child and watch for severe signs. One severe sign means act now.

Response sequence

  1. Lay the child flat. If breathing is difficult, let them sit with legs outstretched. Never let them stand or walk.
  2. Call for the autoinjector and medical bag and bring them to the child.
  3. Give adrenaline into the outer mid-thigh: form a fist, remove the blue safety release, place the orange end against the thigh, push until it clicks, hold for 3 seconds, then remove.
  4. Note the time it was given.
  5. A colleague calls 000. Then phone the family.
  6. If there’s no response after 5 minutes, give a further dose.
Asthma and allergy together, with sudden breathing difficulty? Give the adrenaline autoinjector FIRST, then the asthma reliever. If in doubt, give adrenaline.

Before you use the device

  • Check the child’s name is on the device
  • Check the dose matches the plan (Jr 0.15 mg / 0.3 mg)
  • Check the expiry date
  • Never give antihistamine instead of adrenaline when there are respiratory signs

Regulatory obligations

  • r.136: first aid, anaphylaxis and emergency asthma trained educators in attendance and immediately available at all times
  • r.94: medication can be given without authorisation in an emergency. Notify the parent and emergency services as soon as practicable
  • r.87: complete the incident, injury, trauma and illness record
  • Notify the regulatory authority of the serious incident within 24 hours
Based on ASCIA Action Plans for Anaphylaxis and the Education and Care Services National Regulations. Check against current ASCIA resources and your service’s medical conditions policy.
