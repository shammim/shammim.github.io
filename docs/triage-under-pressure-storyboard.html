1. Project summary

A short micro-learning module for emergency call-takers on a 24/7 roster. It refreshes the first-minute question sequence, practises spotting the priority signals callers bury or play down, then puts the learner on a two-minute simulated live call with caller audio, a call clock and a decision timer.

The module is designed for a shift briefing or a quiet moment between calls: no lecture, every screen maps to a decision the call-taker makes on a live call.

2. Analysis

AreaFinding
Learning needs analysisCall logs, priority dispatch error reports, QA audit scores and supervisor shift debriefs, triangulated to find the behavioural failure point.
Failure pointUnder time pressure, call-takers ask history questions before locking the location, and treat “snoring” in a collapsed patient as reassurance. Both delay the right priority code.
LearnersEmergency call-takers, dispatchers and trainees. High cognitive load, auditory fatigue, rostered across 24/7 shifts.
EnvironmentMulti-monitor CAD workstations, headset-only audio, strict call-duration benchmarks. Learning happens in short windows.
Behaviour targetCode the right priority within 60 seconds: location, callback, chief complaint, then the big two (conscious? breathing normally?).
Business measuresCall processing time, triage accuracy rate and QA protocol compliance, measured before and 30 days after rollout.

3. Design approach

DecisionChoiceWhy
ModalityRise-style micro-learning with an embedded branching call simulationQuick reference for shift briefings, plus timed, audio-driven decisions that feel like the CAD floor.
Action MappingEvery interaction is a call-taking decisionStrips out theory; practice targets the moves that change the outcome.
Cognitive loadThe simulated CAD panel shows only six fieldsLocation, callback, chief complaint, conscious, breathing, priority: nothing that does not drive the decision.
Stress inoculationCaller audio, line noise, a call clock and a 20-second decision timerBuilds procedural confidence under pressure. Timer and sound can be switched off.
AccessibilityWCAG 2.1 AACaptions and transcript for every caller line, keyboard operable, adjustable timing.

4. Learning objectives

By the end, the learner can:

  • Run the first-60-seconds question sequence in order: location, callback number, what happened, the big two, code.
  • Recognise priority signals that callers bury or play down, including agonal breathing (“snoring”).
  • Sort calls into the right priority level.
  • Take a simulated live call and set the priority within 60 seconds, then coach the caller.
