Job aids

Asthma Management: NDIS In-Home Support

Five one-page job aids for disability support workers. One for each lesson of the course.

# Job aid Use it when Where to keep it
1 Signs asthma isn't well controlled A participant seems unwell, tired or less active, or their reliever is running low Shift notes, handover
2 Triggers and choice Planning an outing or a change in the weather, smoke or pollen Participant's support plan folder
3 Puffers, spacers and weekly checks Checking medication, or when you notice a device problem Medication cupboard
4 Reading the action plan A participant's symptoms change, or reliever use goes up Beside the participant's action plan
5 Asthma first aid, working alone A participant is struggling to breathe With the reliever, spacer and action plan

How to use these

  • Print on A4. Keep aid 5 next to each participant's reliever, spacer and action plan, and read it at the start of each first shift with them.

  • Use aids 1 to 4 on shift, at handover and at supervision visits. They support the participant's plan. They don't replace it.

  • Ask your team leader for picture or easy read versions for participants who would benefit.

  • Before roll-out, have your clinical lead check the content against current guidance and your provider's policies.

JOB AID 1 · LESSON 1

Signs asthma isn't well controlled

Small signs, spread across days. Notice them, write them down, report them.

What to look for

Signs Many participants won't say “I'm short of breath”. They may...

• Coughing or waking at night

• Needing the reliever more often than usual

• Avoiding activities that used to be easy

• Stop and sit down

• Seem irritable or tired

• Avoid stairs

Don't write “has a cold”. Write what you see and hear, with times.

Good: “Sat down after 5 minutes of weeding, breathing fast. Said bad night, again. Reliever counter nearly empty.”

What to do

  1. Learn what's normal for each participant, so you notice when it changes.

  2. Check the reliever's dose counter when you check other medications.

  3. Ask in simple words: “Is breathing hard?” Watch for nods, gestures or behaviour.

  4. Report a pattern to your team leader on the same shift, using your notes.

  5. Agree a plan to get the participant's asthma reviewed by their GP.

Do

  • Record facts and times.

  • Tell the team leader it's a pattern.

  • Include the participant: write down what matters to them.

Don't

  • Leave it for the next worker to keep an eye on.

  • Give extra reliever on a schedule. It isn't in the plan, and it hides the problem.

JOB AID 2 · LESSON 2

Triggers and choice

Reduce exposure. The participant still decides.

Common asthma triggers

Trigger Notes
Colds and flu Respiratory infections.
Pollen In Victoria, epidemic thunderstorm asthma can happen in grass pollen season, usually October to December. In spring, check the forecast at the start of your shift.
Smoke Cigarette, bushfire and wood fire.
Cold air, dust and mould
Strong smells Sprays and perfume.
Exercise and stress

Each participant's triggers are different. Check their support plan, and ask them and their family.

The choice conversation

  1. Check the support plan for triggers, and check the forecast or warning.

  2. Explain the risk with plain words, pictures or the participant's preferred communication style.

  3. Offer real choices. For example: go early and be home before the storm, or order for delivery.

  4. Plan for safety whichever they pick: reliever and spacer go too, windows closed before the storm.

  5. Record what the participant decided, and why.

Do

  • Share the risk in a way they understand.

  • Make the reliever and spacer go everywhere the participant goes.

  • Tell your team leader about the warning and the choice.

Don't

  • Cancel without explaining why. It takes away their choice and they may stop telling you their plans.

  • Ignore a known high-risk trigger or leave the reliever at home.

JOB AID 3 · LESSON 3

Puffers, spacers and weekly checks

A medicine only helps if it reaches the lungs

What each item does, and what to check

Item What it does What to check
Preventer Reduces swelling in the airways over time. Taken every day, even when well. Taken as prescribed, mouth rinsed after if the chart says so
Reliever (usually blue or grey) Opens the airways within minutes. Used for symptoms and first aid. In date, dose counter not near zero, always with the participant
Spacer Helps more medicine reach the lungs Clean, drip-dried, not cracked, goes everywhere with the reliever

Spacer care

Warm soapy water, then drip-dry.

Don't dry a spacer with a tea towel. It leaves static inside, so less medicine reaches the lungs.

Your role

Do

  • Follow the medication chart and your provider's medication policy.

  • Only support medication tasks you've been trained and authorised to do.

  • Support the participant to use their spacer and rinse their mouth, as the chart says.

  • Ask for a pharmacist or GP technique check, and a picture guide, if you notice a problem.

Don't

  • Take over a task the participant does themselves, unless their plan says so. It can remove independence and trust.

  • Leave a technique problem, an expired puffer or a missing spacer.

JOB AID 4 · LESSON 4

Reading the action plan

Match what you see to a section. Do what that section says.

Three sections in most plans

Section What it is for
When well (well controlled) Daily medicines.
When getting worse What to do when symptoms increase. For example, Liam's plan says: if he needs his reliever more than usual or wakes at night with asthma, keep taking the preventer and see the GP within a day or two.
Emergency What to do in an emergency. See job aid 5.

Each participant's plan is different. Read theirs, not a general one.

Steps

  1. Know where the plan is kept. Read it at the start of your first shift with the participant.

  2. Look at what you see: reliever use, waking at night, breathing, talking, moving around.

  3. Match it to a section of the plan.

  4. Do what that section says, within the plan's timeframe. Don't wait for a family visit.

  5. Tell your team leader and the participant's supporters when the “getting worse” section applies. Record reliever use.

Do

  • Work around what matters to the participant, such as the cricket, when booking the GP.

  • Ask for an accessible version (pictures, easy read) if it would help.

  • Ask for the plan to be reviewed if it's out of date or hard to follow.

Don't

  • Change a dose unless the plan or the prescriber tells you to. Tell your team leader and get the participant seen.

  • Wait for a more convenient time when the plan gives a timeframe.

JOB AID 5 · LESSON 5

Asthma first aid, working alone

000 and first aid come before calling your team leader

Call 000 straight away if the person

• is not breathing, or gets suddenly worse

• has severe signs, such as only speaking a few words, or blue or pale lips

• has no reliever

• or you're not sure it's asthma

Asthma first aid with a blue or grey reliever puffer

  1. Sit the person upright, stay with them and be calm and reassuring.

  2. Give 4 separate puffs of the reliever through a spacer: 1 puff at a time, with 4 breaths after each puff.

  3. Wait 4 minutes. If they still can't breathe normally, give 4 more puffs.

  4. If they still can't breathe normally, call 000. Keep giving 4 puffs every 4 minutes until the ambulance arrives.

Working alone

  • Use your phone's speaker, so you can call and give first aid at the same time.

  • Know where the reliever, spacer and action plan are at the start of every shift.

  • If the participant's action plan or reliever uses different first aid steps, follow those.

Avoid

  • Calling your team leader first.

  • Lying the person down, or sending several quick puffs one after another without the spacer.

Time log (hand to the paramedics with the action plan)

Participant 000 called at First puff at Puffs given (time of each round)

Afterwards

  • Tell your team leader.

  • Complete an incident report through your provider's system. Your team leader will decide whether it needs to be reported to the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission.

  • Look after yourself. Talk to your team leader or your employee support line.
