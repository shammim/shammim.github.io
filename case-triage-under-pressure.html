Triage Under Pressure

A micro-learning module for emergency call-takers: lock the location, ask the big two and code the call within 60 seconds, on a simulated live call with caller audio and a decision timer.

My role

What I did on this project

Workplace project for an emergency call-taking context. Names, addresses, incident numbers and talkgroups are fictional, and the protocols are simplified for learning. They are not the operational procedures of any emergency service.

Challenge & context

Under shift pressure, call-takers can misclassify a high-priority call or escalate a panicking caller. Two common traps: asking for history before locking the location, and hearing “snoring” in a collapsed patient as reassurance.

Instructional strategy

Action-mapped micro-learning: a baseline instinct check, the five-move question sequence, pressure traps and a sorting activity, then a two-minute live call against the clock and a debrief.

Content & evaluation

Built from a learning needs analysis of call logs, QA audit scores and supervisor debriefs. Kirkpatrick Level 1 and 2 are captured in the module, with Level 3 through a 30-day QA call audit.

Interactive features

A simulated CAD panel with six fields that fill as the call progresses, caller audio with captions, a call clock and a 20-second decision timer, and branching choices that each add seconds to the call.

TU-4.4Live call

The snoring trap

On screen
CAD panel with the call clock. Location and callback are verified; the chief complaint reads “Collapse · ‘snoring’ noise”.
Interaction
Three replies, each with a time cost. The strongest asks whether he is awake; “Snoring is a good sign” is the trap.
Audio
Caller: “He was making tea and he just dropped. Now he’s making this weird snoring noise.” Captions on.
