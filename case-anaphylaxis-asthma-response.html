Challenge & context

Anaphylaxis can become life-threatening within minutes. At the lunch table there is no time to look anything up, so educators need to recognise the signs and follow the ASCIA Action Plan straight away.

Instructional strategy

Context, then sign recognition, then a timed Test Me. The learner plays the lead educator and first aid officer in a preschool room with 22 children and three ASCIA plans.

Content & compliance

Mapped to NQF Regulation 136 (first aid qualifications), ASCIA Action Plans for Anaphylaxis, the anaphylaxis-plus-asthma rule, Regulation 94 and incident records under Regulation 87.

Interactive features

Custom variables, timed decision layers and a falling patient stability score. A wrong call opens a feedback layer, and every step is logged as an xAPI statement.

AN-L2-01Reveal cards

Mild reaction or anaphylaxis?

On screen
One severe sign is enough to act on, even if other signs look mild.

Six sign cards. Selecting each reveals its category; a counter shows "6 of 6 revealed". A summary then links Noah’s symptoms to anaphylaxis.

AN-L3-01Timed simulation

Test Me: 60 seconds to respond

On screen
The clock starts when you select Start. Noah’s stability score drops every second until he gets adrenaline.
Interaction
Three steps: Evaluate symptoms, Locate (scan the First Aid Officer badge to unlock the cabinet), Respond. Variables: Timer_Remaining, Symptoms_Evaluated, Cabinet_Unlocked, Correct_Med, EpiPen_Time, Patient_Stability_Score, Ambulance_Called, Attempts.
Audio
Ticking timer with an audio on/off toggle.

Feedback layer

Classifying the signs as mild, or reaching for the wrong medication, opens a feedback layer that explains the ASCIA rule.
